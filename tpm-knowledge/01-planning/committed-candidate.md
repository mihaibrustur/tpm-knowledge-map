# Committed and candidate scope

Splitting a plan into work the team commits to and work it will pull in if there is room, with capacity held back for interrupts.

## Why it matters

Urgent requests, incidents and maintenance always arrive. Committing only part of capacity (often around three quarters, as a starting hypothesis) keeps promises credible instead of breaking them every cycle.

## Pitfalls

- Committing 100% of capacity and treating every interrupt as a surprise.
- Candidate work that quietly becomes a commitment in leadership's eyes.
- Picking a buffer size once and never checking it against actual interrupts.

## Reflect

- How do you manage unplanned urgent requests in a fixed-capacity team?
- How did you explain a buffer to leaders who wanted everything committed?
