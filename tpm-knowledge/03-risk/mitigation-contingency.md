# Mitigation and contingency

Mitigation lowers the chance or impact of a risk ahead of time; contingency is the plan you switch to if it happens.

## Why it matters

Having both means you are not inventing a response under pressure. A clear trigger for the contingency makes the switch a decision, not a debate.

## Pitfalls

- Contingency plans with no trigger, so nobody knows when to use them.
- Mitigations that cost more than the risk they address.
- Assuming a risk is handled because it has been discussed.

## Reflect

- When did you activate a fallback plan?
- What did a mitigation cost, and was it worth it?
